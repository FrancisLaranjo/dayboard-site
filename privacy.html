Dayboard

Privacy policy

Last updated: 10 October 2026

Who this covers

Dayboard is a desktop app that one person made for their own Windows PC. This policy describes what it does with the Google data it can read.

What Dayboard reads

  • Gmail (read-only access): the list of unread inbox threads (sender, subject, time, Gmail's short snippet and category), Gmail's unread counts, and, only when you click a mail, the plain text and attachment names of its newest message.
  • Google Calendar events (read-only access): the events on your calendars, to show today's.

What Dayboard stores

Dayboard stores one thing from Google on your PC: the sign-in token that lets it keep reading without asking you to sign in each time. It is saved in your own Windows user folder.

Mail and calendar data are kept in the app's memory only while it runs. A mail you open is shown and then discarded; it is never written to disk.

What Dayboard shares

Nothing. Dayboard talks only to Google's own servers to read your data. It has no server of its own, no analytics, no advertising, and it does not sell, share or transfer your data to anyone.

What Dayboard never does

It never sends, deletes, labels, archives or marks mail as read, never changes your calendar, and never loads images or other remote content from an email.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Dayboard's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

You can remove Dayboard's access at any time from your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Once access is removed, the token stored on your PC no longer works.
